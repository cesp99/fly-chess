Play chess against a fruit fly brain

The player is a recurrent neural network whose neurons and synapses are the FlyWire connectome of an adult fruit fly: ~134,000 neurons and ~2.7 million neuron-to-neuron connections (34 million synapses in the wiring diagram), wired exactly as in the animal. Only the synaptic strengths were trained. It is not a chess engine. It is a fly.

    How a fly plays chess

    FlyWire mapped every neuron and synapse in the brain of an adult female Drosophila melanogaster from electron microscopy: about 139,000 neurons and tens of millions of synapses, with the neurotransmitter of most neurons predicted from images.

    We turned that wiring diagram into a recurrent neural network. Each neuron is a unit; each synapse is a connection that exists only if the fly has it. Acetylcholine neurons excite, GABA and glutamate neurons inhibit (Dale's law), and those signs were never learned. What training changed: how strong each existing synapse is, each neuron's bias and time constant, and two small projections — the board is injected into ~2,000 sensory neurons, and the move is read out of ~2,000 descending and motor neurons.

    The board is presented for a few recurrent timesteps; the descending neurons' activity is decoded into a probability over moves (the policy) and a guess of who is winning (the value). The dots beside the board are 2,048 of those neurons, drawn where they sit in the real brain, glowing with their activity; after every fly move the thought replay scrubs through the timesteps so you can watch the wave travel from the eyes through the optic lobes into the central brain and down to the descending neurons.

    It learned by imitating 57 million positions from about 780,000 human games on Lichess (both players rated 1800+). Larva samples the policy. Fly takes the policy's favourite move and checks it one ply ahead with the value head. Superfly runs a Monte-Carlo tree search (400 simulations per move on WebGPU, 40 in plain JavaScript) where every evaluation is the fly brain. Nothing else picks moves.
